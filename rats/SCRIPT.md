# "The Rat Tail Bounty" — Hanoi 1902 (perverse incentives) — script v1

First short of the economics series. Picture in the Emden/sewers style (3D toon sets, 2D cartoon characters).
Script: hook with the paradox in one line → setting (new French sewers) → escalation (plague, sewer crews, 20,000 a day)
→ the "brilliant idea" (a cent per tail) → it "works" → the strange clue (rats with no tails) → "You see," explanation
→ the double twist (rat farms) → the economics word ("a perverse incentive") → the rule in one line → loop ending.
Our own story, characters and shots. Vietnamese and French characters are drawn with the same bean-head style as
everyone else; clothes and hats are the only period markers (no caricature).

## Facts (checked 14/11/2026)
- 1902, Hanoi (French Indochina): the French built a modern sewer system with flush toilets under the European quarter;
  the sewers became ideal rat habitat. Plague (the third pandemic) was spreading in Asia and rats carry it.
  Sources: Michael G. Vann, "Of Rats, Rice, and Race: The Great Hanoi Rat Massacre" (French Colonial History, 2003);
  Wikipedia "Great Hanoi Rat Massacre"; Saigoneer interview with Vann.
- The administration hired Vietnamese crews to go into the sewers and kill rats; kills reached thousands a day, with
  20,114 rats recorded on 12 June 1902 (another account gives 20,112 on 21 June: say "more than twenty thousand").
  Sources: Vann (2003); ScienceBlog summary of Vann.
- The city then paid a bounty to anyone who brought in a rat tail: 1 cent per tail (later 2 cents by end of 1902,
  4 cents by 1904). The tail was the proof, so officials didn't have to handle the bodies. Sources: Wikipedia; Vann.
- Officials saw tailless rats in the streets: people cut off the tails and released the rats to keep breeding.
  Health inspectors found villagers outside Hanoi raising rats for the bounty. The bounty was cancelled.
  Sources: Wikipedia "Great Hanoi Rat Massacre", "Perverse incentive"; Vann.
- "Perverse incentive": an incentive that produces the opposite of what was intended. Source: Wikipedia.
- Not used (unverified): exact plague dates in Hanoi, the "55 million tails" figure, "farmers released their rats".

## Script (English)
The French paid a cent for every dead rat in Hanoi, and the city ended up with more rats than ever.
You see, in 1902, they built shiny new sewers under the French part of town. With flushing toilets, just like Paris.
But to a rat, those sewers were a luxury hotel. Warm, dark, and full of food.
Then plague was spreading across Asia, and rats carry plague.
So they sent crews down into the pipes to kill them. Thousands a day. On one day in June, more than twenty thousand.
But the rats kept coming. So the French had a brilliant idea.
Anyone who brought in a rat tail would get paid one cent. Just the tail, because nobody wanted a pile of dead rats on their desk.
And it worked. Tails poured in by the bucket.
Until officials noticed something strange. Rats running through the streets with no tails.
You see, people were catching rats, cutting off the tail, and letting them go.
A rat with no tail can still have babies. And every baby grows a brand new tail.
Then inspectors found farms outside the city, raising rats on purpose. The French had accidentally invented rat farming.
Economists have a name for this. A perverse incentive.
Pay for rat tails, and you'll get rat tails. Not fewer rats.
So the bounty was cancelled, and the rats kept their luxury hotel. Because...

## Guion (español)
Los franceses pagaban un céntimo por cada rata muerta en Hanói, y la ciudad acabó con más ratas que nunca.
Verás, en 1902 construyeron unas alcantarillas nuevas y relucientes bajo el barrio francés. Con váteres de cisterna, como en París.
Pero para una rata, esas alcantarillas eran un hotel de lujo. Calientes, oscuras y llenas de comida.
Entonces la peste se extendía por Asia, y las ratas transmiten la peste.
Así que mandaron cuadrillas a las tuberías para matarlas. Miles al día. Un día de junio, más de veinte mil.
Pero las ratas seguían llegando. Así que los franceses tuvieron una idea brillante.
Cualquiera que trajera una cola de rata cobraría un céntimo. Solo la cola, porque nadie quería un montón de ratas muertas en su escritorio.
Y funcionó. Llegaban colas a cubos.
Hasta que los funcionarios notaron algo raro. Ratas corriendo por las calles sin cola.
Verás, la gente cazaba ratas, les cortaba la cola y las soltaba.
Una rata sin cola puede seguir teniendo crías. Y a cada cría le crece una cola nueva.
Luego los inspectores encontraron granjas fuera de la ciudad que criaban ratas a propósito. Los franceses habían inventado sin querer la cría de ratas.
Los economistas tienen un nombre para esto. Un incentivo perverso.
Si pagas por colas de rata, tendrás colas de rata. No menos ratas.
Así que cancelaron la recompensa, y las ratas se quedaron con su hotel de lujo. Porque...
