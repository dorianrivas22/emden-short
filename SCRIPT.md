# "The Fake Chimney" — original script v1

Format: vertical short, ~60 s, ~3.7 words/s, channel formula (absurd hook → skeptics → bold move → reveal with
"You see," at ~2/3 → everyday analogy → poetic/punch ending). Our own story, characters and visuals.

## Facts (sources: Wikipedia "SMS Emden (1908)", "Battle of Penang"; Tamil slang "emden")
- SMS Emden, German light cruiser, Captain Karl von Müller, raiding in the Indian Ocean in autumn 1914.
- The crew rigged a dummy fourth funnel (wood and canvas) so the three-funnel Emden looked like a British
  four-funnel cruiser (e.g. HMS Yarmouth).
- 28 October 1914, before dawn: Emden entered Penang harbour (British Malaya) unchallenged, flying no ensign.
- Russian cruiser Zhemchug lay at anchor; her captain was ashore. Emden raised the German ensign before
  opening fire (rules of war), torpedoed and shelled her; Zhemchug sank within minutes in the harbour.
- On the way out Emden also sank the French destroyer Mousquet.
- Emden's exploits (incl. shelling Madras, Sept 1914) made "emden" a Tamil word for a daring, cunning person.
- Ships at a distance / in poor light were identified by silhouette, notably by counting funnels.

## Script
In 1914, a German crew built a fake chimney out of wood and canvas, and bolted it onto their warship.
The sailors thought the captain had lost his mind. The ocean is full of enemy ships, and he's doing arts and crafts.
But he just points at the map. Penang. We're going in.
Penang was a British harbor, packed with enemy warships. Sailing in there was like strolling into a police station wearing a ski mask.
So before sunrise, they cruise straight past the lighthouse. The guards squint, count the chimneys, and wave.
Inside, a Russian cruiser is fast asleep at anchor. Its captain isn't even on board.
The Germans glide right up next to it. Then they raise their flag, and fire.
You see, in the dark, nobody could see a flag. So lookouts counted chimneys. British cruisers had four. The German ship had three.
It's like spotting your friend in a crowd by their red hat. You never check the face.
Minutes later, the Russian cruiser was at the bottom of the harbor, and the Germans were steaming back out to sea.
That ship was called the Emden. And in the Tamil language, calling someone an Emden still means they're way too clever.
